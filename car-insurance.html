What Is Car Insurance?

Car insurance is a contract between you and your insurer: you pay a premium, and the insurer covers specific losses if your vehicle is damaged, stolen or involved in an accident. In Ontario it is a legal requirement under the Compulsory Automobile Insurance Act – driving without it is against the law.

  • Repairs or replacement of your vehicle or another driver’s vehicle
  • Liability costs if you injure someone or damage property
  • Medical and rehabilitation costs after an accident
  • Theft, vandalism, fire and other risks with optional coverages

Mandatory Coverage in Ontario

Every Ontario auto policy must include these four coverages:

1Third-Party Liability

Covers injury or property damage you cause to others. The legal minimum is $200,000 – most drivers choose $1M or $2M.

2Statutory Accident Benefits

Medical treatment, rehabilitation and attendant care after an accident, regardless of who was at fault.

3Uninsured Automobile

Protects you and your family if you’re injured by an uninsured driver or in a hit-and-run.

4Direct Compensation – Property Damage

Your own insurer pays for vehicle damage when another insured driver is at fault.

💡
Broker’s tip on DCPD: Since 2024 you can opt out of DCPD to lower your premium slightly – but then you pay all your own repair costs, even when someone else caused the accident. For most drivers, keeping it is the safer choice.

Optional Coverages

Collision

Pays for repairs to your vehicle after a crash, regardless of fault.

Comprehensive

Covers theft, vandalism, fire and weather-related damage.

Accident Forgiveness

Keeps your premium from rising after your first at-fault accident.

Loss of Use

Pays for a rental car or other transportation while yours is being repaired.

Waiver of Depreciation

Replaces a new car at full value, without depreciation, if it’s written off early.

Optional Accident Benefits

Increase or add benefits such as income replacement – ask your broker what fits you.
